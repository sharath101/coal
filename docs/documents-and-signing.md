# Documents and signing

**Read this when** you work on documents, boilerplates, versions, signatures, step sign-offs, passkeys, files or the audit trail.
**Related:** [workflow.md](./workflow.md) for what each step produces and who signs it, [workstation-and-ai.md](./workstation-and-ai.md#drafting-a-document) for AI drafting, [regulatory-notes.md](./regulatory-notes.md) for what the rules may later require.

⚠ marks an assumption. See [open-questions.md](./open-questions.md#documents-and-signing).

---

## Kinds of documents

| Kind | Examples | How it's made |
|---|---|---|
| **Boilerplate document** | Product concept, PID, Market Research Survey, Action Plan | Made from Jayon's own Word boilerplate. Fields are filled from records, and written sections by a person or the AI. Turned into a PDF for review and signing |
| **Uploaded document** | SolidWorks drawing PDFs and STEP files, FEA reports before their template exists, the lab's TRF and test report, pictures | A person uploads the file. It gets the same versions and signatures as a boilerplate document |
| **Generated record** | The step sign-off record | Generated by COAL from the step's data |

Every document belongs to a project and to the step that produces it. Document *types* (PID, Action Plan and so on) set each one's template, numbering and signature rows.

## Boilerplates

Each boilerplate has two parts:

- **The Word template.** Jayon's own form with placeholders, so the output looks exactly like the documents people and auditors already know.
- **A specification** next to it, saying:
  - which sections are **fields**, filled by code from records (project code, names, dates, team, figures);
  - which sections are **written**, with instructions on what to write and which sources to use;
  - the signature rows.

```yaml
template: PID
version: 3
docx: backend/templates/PID-r3.docx
sections:
  - id: header
    kind: fields                     # filled from records by code, never by the AI
    fields:
      project_code: { from: project.code }
      product_line: { from: project.product_line }
      date:         { from: today }
  - id: purpose
    kind: written                    # written by a person or the AI, with citations
    instructions: State the clinical and market need in 120 to 200 words.
    sources: [concept, "step:2.documents"]
  - id: team
    kind: fields
    fields:
      members: { from: project.team, format: table }
signature_rows: [prepared, reviewed, approved]
```

**How documents are filled.** docxtemplater fills the Word template, Gotenberg turns it into a PDF, and people review the PDF. Fields are edited in forms and written sections in a simple text editor, so nobody can break the layout by accident.

**Template changes.** A new template version applies to new documents. Each document remembers the template version it started from. For now, templates are changed by developers in the repository, with review. Later this becomes controlled in the app.

## Versions and revisions

- **Drafts are numbered** as they are saved: draft 1, 2, 3. Each save is a new version; nothing is overwritten.
- **Revision letters at approval.** A document gets its revision letter (A, B, C) when it's approved. Approved versions are locked: changing one means a new revision that goes through signing again.
- **Hashes.** Every version stores the SHA-256 hash of its content and of its PDF. A signature is tied to those hashes.
- **Compare.** Versions can be compared field by field, and written sections as a text diff.
- **Rework.** When work goes back to an earlier step ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#failure-and-rework)), a document keeps its identity. Its latest version is copied into the new iteration as a new draft, and each version records the step iteration it was made in.
- **Numbering.** Each document type has a numbering pattern. The default is `{project code}-{type}-{nn}` until we have Jayon's convention ⚠.

## Signature rows

A document is signed in up to three rows, in order: **Prepared**, then **Reviewed**, then **Approved**.

- **Prepared:** the doer or doers who submit it.
- **Reviewed:** as in the template table above. Not every document has a reviewer.
- **Approved:** the step's signer ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#the-steps)).
- **Returning.** At any row, the signer can return the document to its authors with comments instead of signing.
- **Separation of duties.** The same person can't sign both Prepared and Approved on the same version.
- **Handover checklist.** At step 24b, the production head signs it with the meaning **Received and accepted**.

Decisions are signed too: Go at step C, and the decisions at checkpoints and at step 12 ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#decisions-recorded-at-steps)).

## Step sign-off records

Every step with a sign-off ends with a **step sign-off record**. COAL generates it when a doer submits the step. It lists:

- the step and its iteration;
- every output, with its document number, version and hash, including uploads;
- any decisions;
- the deviations;
- the dates.

The step's signer signs this record. If the signer is also the Approved row on the step's main document, one Face ID or fingerprint prompt signs both.

This gives every sign-off something concrete to sign, including steps that produce no named document, such as steps 6 and 9.

## Signing with Face ID or fingerprint

Signatures use **passkeys**, the standard behind Face ID, fingerprint and Windows Hello sign-in on websites and apps. The face or fingerprint never leaves the device; COAL never sees it.

- **Register once.** The first time someone signs, they register a passkey on their phone or laptop. They can register more than one device.
- **Confirm every time.** Each signature asks for Face ID or a fingerprint again. Being signed in isn't enough. If the device's biometrics aren't available, the device's own PIN is accepted instead.
- **What is signed.** The server prepares a **signing intent**:
  - the record and its version;
  - the content hash and the PDF hash;
  - the signer;
  - the meaning (Prepared, Reviewed, Approved…), and the decision and reason, if any;
  - a one-time challenge.

  The device signs it with the passkey, and the server checks the result against the registered public key.
- **What is kept.** The full passkey response is kept with the signature, so any signature can be re-checked later. If the document changes by even one character, the signature no longer matches.
- **What is shown.** The name, role, meaning, and date and time in Indian time appear on screen and on the PDF.
- **Nothing can be changed afterwards.** Signatures and the audit trail can only be added to.
- **Web and mobile.** Signing works in the browser and in the mobile app.
- **A lost device.** Remove its passkey from your profile, then register a new one.

> Until real sign-in exists, anyone can register a passkey on anyone's account. Signatures prove which device signed, not which person. They are for building and testing only ([roles-and-access.md](./roles-and-access.md#accounts-for-now)).

## Audit trail

- **What is recorded.** Every change, signature, decision and sign-in is recorded:
  - who did it: a user, a user via the AI, or the system;
  - what changed, with the values before and after;
  - when, and the reason where one is given.
- **Same transaction.** The entry is written in the same database transaction as the change. If it can't be written, the change doesn't happen.
- **Add-only and chained.** The database account the app uses can't change or delete entries. Each entry stores the hash of the one before, so a removed or altered entry breaks the chain, and a daily job checks the chain.
- **Visible history.** Anyone who can see a record can see its history on the record's Activity tab.

## Files, pictures and sources

- **Storage.** Files are kept in private object storage. They are reached only through short-lived links issued after a permission check.
- **Uploads.** Uploads are scanned for viruses before anyone can open them, and there's a list of allowed file types and a size limit. Outside documents are marked "outside" ([workstation-and-ai.md](./workstation-and-ai.md#safety-basics)).
- **Pictures** can be attached to any step, task or document, including straight from a phone camera.
- **Sources** (a link, a file or a citation, with a note) can be added to any document or step. They show in a Sources section.

## Later

These are deliberately left out until we know more. See [regulatory-notes.md](./regulatory-notes.md) for why some of them may become necessary.

- **Sealed PDFs.** The company's certificate and a trusted timestamp, on final PDFs.
- **Stronger signatures where the law needs them.** Aadhaar eSign or a Digital Signature Certificate for the document types an Indian lawyer names, for example the Board approvals.
- **The steps before signing.** An identity check, an e-signature agreement and training before anyone can sign. These are needed once real sign-in exists.
- **Signing on someone's behalf** during leave, with limits.
- **More signature meanings:** Verified, for a second person checking typed readings, and Attended, Trained and others.
- **Document control:** controlled copies, training on new revisions, effective dates, and archival PDFs (PDF/A).
- **Word copies** with tracked changes, for the record.
- **Locked storage:** write-once storage for approved files, and a daily time-stamped anchor for the audit trail.
- **Retention and disposal** rules.
- **Managing templates and reviewers in the app,** instead of in the repository.
