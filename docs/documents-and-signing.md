# Documents and signing

**Read this when** you work on documents, the template library, versions, signatures, step sign-offs, passkeys, files or the audit trail.
**Related:** [workflow.md](./workflow.md) for what each step produces and who signs it, [workstation-and-ai.md](./workstation-and-ai.md#drafting-a-document) for AI drafting, [regulatory-notes.md](./regulatory-notes.md) for what the rules may later require.

⚠ marks an assumption. See [open-questions.md](./open-questions.md#documents-and-signing).

---

## Kinds of documents

| Kind | Examples | How it's made |
|---|---|---|
| **Template document** | Project Initiation Document, Market Research Survey, Regulatory plan, Project Planning, Project Approval Form, Action Plan, Budget annex, and each step's documents as their templates arrive | Made from Jayon's own Word template in the [template library](#the-template-library). Fields are filled from records or from the person's answers, and written sections by a person or the AI. Turned into a PDF for review and signing |
| **Uploaded document** | SolidWorks drawing PDFs and STEP files, documents whose template hasn't arrived yet, the lab's TRF and test report, pictures | A person uploads the file. It gets the same versions and signatures as a template document |
| **Generated record** | The step sign-off record | Generated by COAL from the step's data |

Every document belongs to a project and to the step that produces it, and is made while that step is open. Document *types* set each one's numbering, signature rows and sensitivity.

### Document types

Types are data (`backend/src/modules/templates/catalogue.ts`), loaded on deploy. Numbers read `{project code}-{abbreviation}-{nn}` ⚠ (S2), for example `SPN-26-001-PID-01`, counting the project's documents of that type.

| Type | Code | Step | Kind | Number | Signed by (after the author) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project Initiation Document | `PID` | 1 | template | `…-PID-nn` | Reviewed: DARD ⚠; Approved: the step's signer (VP) |
| Market Research Survey | `MRS` | 2 | template | `…-MRS-nn` | Reviewed: R&D Lead ⚠; Approved: VP |
| Regulatory plan | `REG_PLAN` | 2a | template | `…-REG-nn` | Nobody: step 2a has no sign-off yet ⚠, so the author's signature is the last |
| Project Planning | `PROJECT_PLANNING` | 3 | template | `…-PLN-nn` | Approved: VP |
| Project Approval Form | `APPROVAL_FORM` | 3 | template | `…-PAF-nn` | Approved: VP |
| Action Plan | `ACTION_PLAN` | 5 | template | `…-ACT-nn` | Reviewed: DARD ⚠; Approved: VP |
| Budget annex | `BUDGET_ANNEX` | 5 | template, **budget** | `…-BUD-nn` | Approved: VP |
| Other document | `DOC` | any | upload | `…-DOC-nn` | Approved: the step's signer |
| Step sign-off record | `STEP_SIGNOFF` | any with a sign-off | record | `…-SO-nn` | Approved: the step's signer, by approving the step |

The product concept at step C isn't a document yet: Go records the idea's name and description ⚠ (S10). A template for each of the other steps' documents is a new type, prepared when Jayon's sample documents arrive.

## The template library

A **template** is Jayon's own Word form (its boilerplate) with placeholders, plus a **specification**. The founders provide a template for each step's documents. COAL keeps them in its **template library**, and the AI checks the library every time it makes a document.

The specification says:

- which document type and steps the template is for;
- which sections are **fields**, and where each field comes from:
  - a record in COAL (project code, names, dates, team);
  - the person, typed in a form (or asked in the chat), when COAL doesn't hold it;
- which sections are **written**, with instructions on what to write and which sources to use;
- the signature rows, and who signs Reviewed and Approved.

```yaml
template: Project Initiation Document
document_type: PID
steps: ["1"]
sections:
  - id: header
    kind: fields                         # filled by code, never by the AI
    fields:
      project_code: { from: project.code, label: Project code }
      date:         { from: today }
  - id: purpose
    kind: written                        # written by a person or the AI
    title: Purpose and clinical need
    instructions: State the clinical and market need in 120 to 200 words.
    sources: [concept]
    required: true
  - id: markets
    kind: fields
    fields:
      target_markets: { from: person, required: true }   # typed by the person, never guessed
  - id: team
    kind: fields
    fields:
      members: { from: project.team }    # a table: {#members}{name}{role}{/members}
signature_rows: [prepared, reviewed, approved]
reviewed_by: [dard]                      # role keys; empty means the Reviewed row isn't used
approved_by: []                          # empty: the step's signer
```

**Where a field comes from** (`from`): `project.code`, `project.name`, `project.description`, `project.product_line`, `project.stage`, `project.team` (a table), `step.key`, `step.name`, `document.number`, `document.title`, `author.name`, `today` (in Indian time) or `person`. The specification schema is `TemplateSpec` in `shared/src/rules/templates.ts`; a name is unique across the whole template.

**The Word file.** Each field is a `{name}` placeholder, each written section a `{section_id}`, and the team table a loop (`{#members}{name}{role}{/members}`, in one table row). Placeholders may sit in the body, headers and footers. Blank lines and line breaks in written sections are kept. Because fields can come from the person, every step's documents can use templates now, including steps whose data COAL doesn't model yet ([data-model.md](./data-model.md#not-modelled-yet-on-purpose)).

### How documents are filled

docxtemplater fills the Word template, Gotenberg turns it into a PDF, and people review the PDF. Fields are edited in forms and written sections in a simple text editor, so nobody can break the layout by accident.

- Fields from COAL's records are filled afresh at every save. A person can't type over them, and the AI never writes them.
- What a person doesn't change stays as it was in the previous version.
- The PDF is made while the version is saved. If the PDF service is down, the save fails and nothing is saved, so a version never exists without its PDF.

### Where templates live

- **In COAL, not in the code repository.** The Word file is kept in file storage, and the specification, versions and status in the database. Templates can change without a code release, and Jayon's documents stay out of the repository.
- **Before they're added,** the founders keep the source files in the `private/templates/` folder. Git ignores that folder, so the files are never committed; share them between computers privately.

### Adding a template

Only the **VP** and the **R&D Head** add and activate templates ⚠ (S6). They do it on the **Templates** page of the website, which is linked from the top bar and the Dashboard for them.

1. **Add.** Choose the document type and upload a new Word (`.docx`) file, with an optional note. A new version **reuses the previous version's specification**, so a new Word file is all the VP or the Head needs. (A type's first template, or a change to its specification, is prepared from Jayon's sample document: by the assistant in the Workstation, which puts the placeholders into the person's own Word file and shows an action card ([the flow](./workstation-and-ai.md#making-a-template-from-a-sample-document)), or by the AI coding assistant for a whole folder at once and the import command below. The checks, and the draft status, are the same.)
2. **Check.** COAL refuses the file, listing every problem to fix, unless:
   - it is a real Word file, no larger than 10 MB;
   - the virus scan finds nothing, where a scanner is set up;
   - its placeholders can be read (no unclosed `{`);
   - its placeholders **match the specification both ways**: none that the specification doesn't define, none missing, tables written as loops with only their own columns;
   - the specification names real roles and steps the type belongs to;
   - a sample PDF, made from made-up data, renders.
3. **Draft.** A file that passes is saved as a **draft** version. Nothing about the active template changes.
4. **Activate.** The VP or the Head makes the draft active. They may activate their own upload ⚠ (S9). Each document type has at most one active version; the previous one is **retired, and kept**. A retired version can be activated again.

The **Templates page** lists every template by document type, with each version's status, steps, who added it and when, who activated it, and links to its sample PDF and its Word file. Everyone else sees only the active templates.

**The import command** loads forms from a folder, each as `NAME.docx` with `NAME.spec.yaml` beside it, and saves them as drafts:

```
pnpm --filter @coal/backend templates:import               # private/templates/
pnpm --filter @coal/backend templates:import path/to/forms
```

### Sample templates

COAL ships **made-up sample templates** for every document type that uses a template, so the whole flow can be tried before Jayon's documents arrive. The backend loads them into the local database when it starts, and `templates:import --samples` loads them anywhere. They are ordinary templates, with plain layouts and made-up wording; Jayon's forms replace them as new versions.

### Template versions and the AI

- **The assistant writes only what it read, and says so.** A draft it saves is checked (numbers from a source, the person's fields in their own words), and the sections it wrote are marked "Written by the assistant" until a person edits them or the version is approved ([drafting](./workstation-and-ai.md#drafting-a-document)).
- **The AI checks every time.** When asked for a document, the AI loads the document type's active template at that moment, never from memory or an earlier chat ([workstation-and-ai.md](./workstation-and-ai.md#drafting-a-document)). A newly activated version is used straight away. If there's no template, the AI says so.
- **Documents keep their version.** A new template version applies to new documents. Each document records the template version it was made from, and its signature rows are fixed when it is made, so a later template change can't alter them.

### Reviewers and approvers

Each template's specification names its signature rows. Once a template is in the library, its specification is the source. This table records what's known before the templates arrive, and what the sample templates use:

| Step | Document | Reviewed by ⚠ | Approved by |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | Product concept | — | — (the VP's Go is a signed decision) |
| 1 | Project Initiation Document | DARD | VP |
| 2 | Market Research Survey | R&D Lead | VP |
| 2a | Regulatory plan | — | — (step 2a has no sign-off yet) |
| 3 | Project Planning; Project Approval Form | — | VP |
| 5 | Action Plan | DARD | VP |

## Versions and revisions

- **Drafts are numbered** as they are saved: draft 1, 2, 3. Each save is a new version; nothing is overwritten. Saving what is already saved makes no new version.
- **Revision letters at approval.** A document gets its revision letter (A, B, C…) when its last signature is made. Approved versions are locked: changing one means a new version that goes through signing again, and approving it supersedes the earlier revision.
- **Locked while with the signers.** A version that has been submitted can't be edited. A signer can return it with a comment, and the authors save a new version.
- **Hashes.** Every version stores the SHA-256 hash of its content (canonical JSON for a template document, the file's bytes for an upload) and of its PDF. A signature is tied to those hashes. The database refuses changes to a saved version's content, files and hashes, and refuses deletes.
- **Compare.** Two versions of a template document can be compared: fields side by side, and written sections as a word-by-word text difference.
- **Rework.** When work goes back to an earlier step ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#failure-and-rework)), a document keeps its identity. Its latest version is copied into the new iteration as a **new draft**, with the same content and PDF, marked carried forward. Each version records the step iteration it was made in. A version that was with its signers is returned first.
- **Numbering.** Each document type has a numbering pattern ([document types](#document-types)) until we have Jayon's convention ⚠.
- **Uploaded documents** have the same versions: each new file is a new version, and its hash is the file's.

## Signature rows

A document is signed in up to three rows, in order: **Prepared**, then **Reviewed**, then **Approved**.

- **Prepared:** the person who submits it, by submitting it. They must be someone who does the step.
- **Reviewed:** anyone holding one of the template's `reviewed_by` roles, within the project's scope ([roles-and-access.md](./roles-and-access.md#content-is-need-to-know)). Not every document has a reviewer.
- **Approved:** the step's signer ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#the-steps)), or the roles the template names. At a step the whole Board must sign (4 and 23), every Board member signs.
- **A row nobody can sign** (Approved on a step with no sign-off, such as 2a) isn't used: the author's signature is the last, and the version is approved when it is submitted.
- **Order.** A row opens when the one before it is signed. A signer sees what waits for them under "Waiting for my signature" on the My tasks page.
- **Returning.** At any row, the signer can return the document to its authors with comments instead of signing. The other rows are cancelled, and the authors save a new version.
- **Separation of duties.** The same person can't sign both Prepared and Approved on the same version of a document. A step's sign-off record follows the route instead: where the route lets one person both do and sign a step (the Lead at step 14), they can sign both rows ⚠ ([W21](./open-questions.md#workflow)).
- **Previews.** Submitting and signing both accept `?preview=true`, which shows the rows and any warning (for example "Nobody holds the DARD role yet") and changes nothing.
- **Until passkeys,** a signature is a confirmed click by the right person ([below](#signing-with-face-id-or-fingerprint)). It is recorded insert-only, with the person's name, role, the meaning, the time and both hashes.
- **Handover checklist.** At step 24b, the production head signs it with the meaning **Received and accepted**. Not built yet: it arrives with the step's own modules ⚠.

**Decisions are signed too.** Every decision is signed by the person who made it, with a hash of exactly what was decided: Go at step C, the checkpoint decisions, the two answers at step 12 ([workflow.md](./workflow.md#decisions-recorded-at-steps)), and hold, resume and drop. A send-back is recorded and audited, with its reason, but isn't signed.

## Step sign-off records

Every step with a sign-off gets a **step sign-off record** (document type `STEP_SIGNOFF`). COAL generates it when a doer submits the step, and makes a new version each time the step is submitted. It lists:

- the project, the step and its iteration;
- every output, with its document number, version, status and hash, including uploads (but not a budget annex, which the whole team would otherwise read about). A document that isn't approved yet is listed as it stands, and the submit preview warns about it ⚠ (S7);
- any decisions made at the step;
- the deviations (none are recorded until the Dashboard block adds them);
- the dates the step started and was submitted.

The record is signed **Prepared** by the person who submitted, and **Approved** by the step's signer *when they approve the step* (every Board member at steps 4 and 23). It can't be signed on its own. If the signer returns the step, the record is returned with it; when the step is submitted again a new version is made. A step with no sign-off (C, 2a, 24a) has none.

This gives every sign-off something concrete to sign, including steps that produce no named document, such as steps 6 and 9. One approval of the step signs the record; a separate click signs a step's main document, until passkeys make one prompt sign both.

## Signing with Face ID or fingerprint

**Built last** ([D16](./decisions.md)): after the Workstation and AI, as the final block of the build. Until then a signature is a **confirmed click** by the right person. Everything around the signature is already in place: the rows, who may sign each, the hashes, the insert-only record (its `method` is `click`), and the sign-off records. The website says "Signing is a confirmed click for now" wherever someone signs. Adding passkeys changes how a signature is *made* (a device prompt, with the server checking the answer), not what a signature *is*.

Signatures will use **passkeys**, the standard behind Face ID, fingerprint and Windows Hello sign-in on websites and apps. The face or fingerprint never leaves the device; COAL never sees it.

- **Register once.** The first time someone signs, they register a passkey on their phone or laptop. They can register more than one device.
- **Confirm every time.** Each signature asks for Face ID or a fingerprint again. Being signed in isn't enough. If the device's biometrics aren't available, the device's own PIN is accepted instead.
- **What is signed.** The server prepares a **signing intent**:
  - the record and its version;
  - the content hash and the PDF hash;
  - the signer;
  - the meaning (Prepared, Reviewed, Approved…), and the decision and reason, if any;
  - a one-time challenge.

  The device signs it with the passkey, and the server checks the result against the registered public key.
- **What is kept.** The full passkey response is kept with the signature, so any signature can be re-checked later. If the document changes by even one character, the signature no longer matches.
- **What is shown.** The name, role, meaning, and date and time in Indian time appear on screen and on the PDF.
- **Nothing can be changed afterwards.** Signatures and the audit trail can only be added to.
- **On the website.** Signing works in the browser: Face ID or Touch ID on Apple devices, Windows Hello, or a fingerprint on Android. On a computer without these, the person can use their phone by scanning a QR code. The mobile app, when it comes, uses the same passkeys.
- **Tied to COAL's web address.** Passkeys work on `localhost` while testing. Passkeys made on a demo host are registered again once the final domain is set ([architecture.md](./architecture.md#sign-in-and-sessions)).
- **A lost device.** Remove its passkey from your profile, then register a new one.

> Until real sign-in exists, anyone can register a passkey on anyone's account. Signatures prove which device signed, not which person. They are for building and testing only ([roles-and-access.md](./roles-and-access.md#accounts-for-now)).

## Audit trail

- **What is recorded.** Every change, signature, decision and sign-in is recorded:
  - who did it: a user, a user via the AI, or the system;
  - what changed, with the values before and after;
  - when, and the reason where one is given.
- **Same transaction.** The entry is written in the same database transaction as the change. If it can't be written, the change doesn't happen.
- **Add-only and chained.** The database account the backend uses can't change or delete entries. Each entry stores the hash of the one before, so a removed or altered entry breaks the chain, and a daily job checks the chain.
- **Visible history.** Anyone who can see a record can see its history on the record's Activity tab.

## Files, pictures and sources

- **Storage.** Files are kept in private object storage. They are reached only through short-lived links issued after a permission check.
- **Who can open a file.** The person who uploaded it, and anyone who may see a record that holds it: a document version's PDF or upload, an attachment or source, a template's sample PDF (and its Word file, for the VP and the Head). Each of those follows its own record's rule ([roles-and-access.md](./roles-and-access.md#content-is-need-to-know)), so a budget annex's attachment is as private as the annex.
- **Uploads.** There's a list of allowed file types and a size limit ⚠ ([I9](./open-questions.md#it-and-data)). Outside documents are marked "outside" ([workstation-and-ai.md](./workstation-and-ai.md#safety-basics)).
- **Virus scanning** uses ClamAV (`CLAMAV_HOST`), and is **required in production**:
  - an upload is scanned when it is completed. An infected file can't be opened, attached or used as a template, and COAL's audit trail records the scan;
  - if the scanner can't be reached, the file stays "not scanned", and a job tries again every minute;
  - where a scanner is set up, a file that isn't scanned yet can't be opened;
  - *Locally,* no scanner runs unless you start one (`docker compose --profile scan up -d`, then set `CLAMAV_HOST=localhost`). Uploads then stay "not scanned" and can be opened. That's acceptable only while COAL holds made-up data. Files COAL makes itself (PDFs) need no scan.
- **Pictures** can be attached to any step, task or document, including straight from a phone camera. An attachment belongs to a project and to exactly one step, document or task, with an optional caption.
- **Sources** (a link, a file or a citation, with a note) can be added to a step, a document or a task, and show in a Sources section. A link must be `http` or `https`.
- **Who can add and read.** The people who do or sign a step, and the project team, add attachments and sources to it. Reading follows the step's or the document's rule; the Board sees a step's attachments only at its checkpoints.

## Later

These are deliberately left out until we know more. See [regulatory-notes.md](./regulatory-notes.md) for why some of them may become necessary.

- **Sealed PDFs.** The company's certificate and a trusted timestamp, on final PDFs.
- **Stronger signatures where the law needs them.** Aadhaar eSign or a Digital Signature Certificate for the document types an Indian lawyer names, for example the Board approvals.
- **The steps before signing.** An identity check, an e-signature agreement and training before anyone can sign. These are needed once real sign-in exists.
- **Signing on someone's behalf** during leave, with limits.
- **More signature meanings:** Verified, for a second person checking typed readings, and Attended, Trained and others.
- **Document control:** controlled copies, training on new revisions, effective dates, and archival PDFs (PDF/A).
- **Word copies** with tracked changes, for the record.
- **Locked storage:** write-once storage for approved files, and a daily time-stamped anchor for the audit trail.
- **Retention and disposal** rules.
- **Template help from the AI:** turning a raw Jayon form into a template, by proposing its placeholders and sections for a person to confirm.
