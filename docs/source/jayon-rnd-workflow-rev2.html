The whole route

Six stages, start to finish, in one picture.

At a sign-off the signer can approve the work or send it back to its author for rework, and a project can be dropped. Every document is signed online. The Managing Director and the CEO sign only as the Board, at steps 4 and 23.

When something fails
  • The FEA fails: The R&D Lead has the design reworked.
  • A prototype fails: The R&D Head sends it back to design (step 7 for an implant, step 8 for an instrument).
  • A lab test fails: R&D Ops, with the help of DARD, takes the first decision on whether the result is a failure. That decision is then presented to the R&D Lead and the R&D Head. If it is a failure, the work goes back to implant design (step 7).
  • The prototype is not approved (step 18): The work can go back as far as step 7, depending on the severity.
  • Design changes are required after the clinical trials (steps 20 and 21): The work starts again from the implant prototype (step 10, the start of stage 3).

Planned across every step

These apply to the whole route, not to one step.

  • SourcesPeople can add the sources behind their research.
  • SignaturesEvery document is signed online, on three rows: Prepared, Reviewed and Approved.
  • Work messagesEach person gets work messages written for their own responsibilities.
  • PicturesPictures can be uploaded at any step.
  • Dates and alertsEach role gets its own dates. An alert goes out when a date is not met, and every deviation is recorded with its reason and who caused it.
  • CostingThe machine log feeds the software, which works out each prototype's cost (manpower, raw material, machine cost and overhead) and saves it as a document.
  • CADAI is integrated into the CAD modelling.
  • Risk fileThe software keeps the risk file, which several of the company's documents refer to.
